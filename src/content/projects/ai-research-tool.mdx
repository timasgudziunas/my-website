## Context

I was spending 2–3 hours reading academic papers for a side project and most of that time was skimming abstracts and hunting for the methodology section. I built a small Chrome extension to fix that for myself.

After sharing it with a few friends, I realized the problem was more universal.

## What I Built

A Chrome extension that reads the active tab, sends it to a Claude-backed API, and returns a structured summary: core claim, method, key findings, and known limitations. It also surfaces papers that contradict the main finding.

## What I Learned

- Browser extension architecture is more constrained than I expected — content scripts, service workers, and message passing have sharp edges
- The hardest part wasn't the AI; it was deciding what the output format should look like
- Shipping something for yourself first removes the paralysis of trying to please everyone
